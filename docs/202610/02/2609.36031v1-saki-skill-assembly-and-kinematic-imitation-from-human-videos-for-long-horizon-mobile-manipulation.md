## 摘要
从人类视频中学习为获取多样化的操作技能提供了一条有前景的途径。将这一能力从桌面场景扩展到长时程移动操作，需要适应并组合跨变化场景和机器人配置所演示的交互。我们提出技能组装与运动学模仿（SAKI），一个连接人类视频技能获取、跨演示组装和闭环全身执行的框架。SAKI 准备可复用的以物体为中心的技能，这些技能保留任务关键交互，同时允许迁移路径自适应。给定目标和提供的任务依赖关系，它选择并排序技能，将其物体角色绑定到当前场景，并在连续技能之间传递场景估计和机器人配置。全身运动学模仿生成协调的底座、手臂和夹爪运动。在执行过程中，持久物体估计在视点变化中维持任务参考，同时视觉反馈更新剩余轨迹。真实机器人实验展示了跨布局的技能复用，以及将独立演示的交互组合为连续移动任务，包括整理和擦拭。消融结果表明，在全身优化和视觉反馈保持不变的情况下，任务条件化的参考准备显著提高了长时程任务完成度。请访问 https://aus.bot/research/saki/ 查看视频演示！

## Abstract
Learning from human videos offers a promising route to acquiring diverse manipulation skills. Extending this capability beyond tabletop settings to long-horizon mobile manipulation requires adapting and composing demonstrated interactions across changing scenes and robot configurations. We present Skill Assembly and Kinematic Imitation (SAKI), a framework connecting human-video skill acquisition, cross-demonstration assembly and closed-loop whole-body execution. SAKI prepares reusable object-centric skills that preserve task-critical interactions while allowing transfer paths to adapt. Given a goal and supplied task dependencies, it selects and orders skills, binds their object roles to the current scene, and carries scene estimates and robot configuration between successive skills. Whole-body kinematic imitation generates coordinated base, arm and gripper motion. During execution, persistent object estimates maintain task references across viewpoint changes, while visual feedback updates remaining trajectories. Real-robot experiments demonstrate skill reuse across layouts and the composition of independently demonstrated interactions into continuous mobile tasks, including tidying and wiping. Ablation results show that task-conditioned reference preparation substantially improves long-horizon task completion with whole-body optimisation and visual feedback held fixed.
