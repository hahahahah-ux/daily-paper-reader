# ABSTRACT 

World Action Models (WAM) improve robot policies by augmenting sparse action supervision with dense future prediction. However, much of the predictable future is dominated by appearance and scene persistence rather than action-dependent dynamics. We observe that several recent WAM designs, including optical flow, motion-centric representations, and latent actions, can be understood from a com-mon perspective in which world supervision becomes more efficient as it contains a higher proportion of action-relevant variation. Based on this insight, we in-troduce Action Tangent Fields, which reformulate world supervision through a local Taylor expansion of how actions induce changes in future dynamics. We represent future dynamics in Residual-VAE space, where the future latent re-mains recoverable from the current latent and its residual, and use a strong action-conditioned world model (ACWM) to probe the local correspondence between action variations and residual-world variations. This local first-order structure is distilled into the WAM to guide its denoising supervision toward dynamics that are more tightly coupled to action, rather than merely predictable from appear-ance. Across LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin, and RoboTwin2.0-Plus, our method con-sistently improves robustness to lighting, background, camera, layout, and other environmental perturbations. Despite using no large-scale embodied pretraining, it achieves stronger robustness under several distribution shifts than pretrained policies. We further distill multi-step VideoDiT denoising into a single step for efficient inference. Our results suggest that effective WAM supervision should re-main information-rich while concentrating its predictive capacity on the directions along which actions change the future.

1arXiv:2610.09734v1 [cs.RO] 7 Oct 2026 1 INTRODUCTION 

Behavior cloning labels each demonstrated observation with an action. World Action Models (WAMs) also predict the future video, giving dense supervision for object motion, contact, and state changes that a single action label cannot describe. A video target, however, rewards predictability rather than control relevance. Backgrounds, camera views, and untouched objects are already deter-mined by the current frame and persist as the robot acts, so a model that reproduces them reduces its loss substantially while learning little about the consequences of its own actions. The value of world supervision therefore depends less on how much of the future it covers than on how much of that future is action-dependent. Recent WAM designs change the target itself. Optical-flow and motion-centric targets keep only what moves, and latent-action targets compress the future into a compact code. Both reduce the supervision spent on static appearance, which is why they help, but both rely on motion as a proxy for action-dependence: a lighting change or a moving distractor alters the future with no contribution from the robot, and dropping appearance removes cues that contact-rich manipulation needs. Achange of target also cannot reveal which directions of the future respond to the action, because each demonstration records one action and the single future that followed it. It is an isolated point of the action–dynamics relation, and its local neighborhood is never observed. Our first step therefore keeps the target information-rich and changes its coordinate instead. Given the current frame, we subtract a broadcast reference derived from it, so the residual describes how the scene departs from the observed frame rather than restating it. Reference and residual recover the future VAE latent exactly, so no information is lost, and because the reference depends only on the current frame, subtracting it leaves the response to an action perturbation unchanged. The second step supplies the missing neighborhood through a probe. For a fixed scene, an embodiment-specific action-conditioned world model (ACWM) is queried at small, temporally smooth perturbations of the expert action. The resulting residual futures differ mainly along direc-tions the ACWM treats as action-sensitive, and their normalized differences span an Action Tangent Field (ATF), a low-dimensional estimate of those directions. Post-training keeps every element of the demonstrated residual in the loss and adds weight along the ATF, which makes the world loss anisotropic, ordinary world post-training is the isotropic special case. The probe sets the weighting, never the targets. We first mid-train the Wan VideoDiT on a modest amount of robot manipulation video to adapt its prediction space to the residual latent space, then post-train a Fast-WAM-style policy with cached ATFs. Caching keeps the ACWM out of policy training and inference. Extensive experiments show that supervising dynamics-relevant world representations enables our method to generalize to visual perturbations substantially better than competing methods. Our contributions are as follows. • We introduce an information-preserving Residual-VAE target that shifts world prediction toward scene dynamics while retaining exact recovery of the future video. • Motivated by a first-order Taylor expansion of the action-to-future dynamics map, we intro-duce Action Tangent Fields to estimate locally action-responsive directions and emphasize them during denoising supervision of the real demonstrated future. • We obtain efficient world features with one VideoDiT evaluation at inference. Aggregate success improves from 51.5% to 87.8% over Fast-WAM on LIBERO-Plus and from 72.7% to 79.3% on RoboTwin 2.0-Plus. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 WORLD ACTION MODELS 

World Action Models (WAMs) complement sparse action labels with future prediction, encouraging policies to retain object states, interactions, and temporal dynamics. Predict-then-act methods such as GE-Act generate compact visual futures and recover controls through inverse dynamics, while GE-Act 2.0 scales this design with heterogeneous interaction data (Liao et al., 2025). Joint models 2instead couple world and action generation: LingBot-VA autoregressively interleaves both outputs, whereas Cosmos Policy represents future observations, actions, and values as latent frames of a video diffusion model (Li et al., 2026c; Kim et al., 2026). A complementary line treats prediction primarily as representation supervision. Fast-WAM and Light-WAM retain video co-training but omit explicit generation at deployment (Yuan et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026d), whereas Efficient-WAM and Faster-WAM preserve future latents while reducing generation or video–action interaction costs (Li et al., 2026b; Zhao et al., 2026). The prediction tar-get nevertheless determines how model capacity is allocated: full video latents preserve rich details but are dominated by static appearance and other variations weakly related to control, whereas flow, residual, and motion-centric targets emphasize change but may discard useful cues. In contrast, Action Tangent Fields retain an information-rich residual future space and selectively emphasize locally action-responsive directions. 2.2 ACTION -C ONDITIONED WORLD MODELS 

Action-conditioned world models (ACWMs) predict visual consequences under specified controls. Recent systems broaden the control interface: Cosmos 3 jointly models language, images, video, audio, and actions (NVIDIA Cosmos Team, 2026), Hydra-0 represents actions as pixel-space flow for cross-embodiment transfer (Li et al., 2026a), and Pelican-Sim combines a unified numerical action space with rendered action videos (Zou et al., 2026). These methods condition generation through native action tokens, visual motion, and action-rendered observations, respectively. Another line improves simulation reliability. ABot-PhysWorld uses physics-aware data and prefer-ence optimization to suppress implausible interactions (Chen et al., 2026b). Ctrl-World combines multi-view prediction, frame-level action conditioning, and pose-aware memory for policy-in-the-loop rollouts (Guo et al., 2026), while WALL-SS uses coarse-to-fine autoregression and compressed memory for long streaming generation (Zhang et al., 2026a). WorldSync addresses failures on off-expert commands through broader action coverage, dynamics grounding, and intervention-effect alignment (Chen et al., 2026a). Unlike these approaches, we neither plan with generated rollouts nor use synthetic futures as supervision targets. Instead, we perturb demonstrated actions and use the ACWM as a local differential probe in Residual-VAE space, transferring the resulting first-order action–world geometry while retaining real demonstrations as prediction targets. 

# 3 METHOD 

We introduce ∆WAM to train a world-action policy on real future video while transferring local action sensitivity from an action-conditioned world model (ACWM). Figure 2 outlines the method. We first adapt Wan to predict future video latents relative to the current frame (Wan Team et al., 2025). We then perturb demonstrated actions and query an embodiment-specific ACWM (NVIDIA Cosmos Team, 2026) to extract action tangent fields (ATFs), which identify locally action-responsive directions. Finally, we post-train a Fast-WAM-style VideoDiT–ActionDiT policy (Yuan et al., 2026) on real action–future pairs, giving additional weight to prediction errors along those directions. At inference, one VideoDiT pass provides the world features used to generate an action chunk. 3.1 RESIDUAL VIDEO MID -T RAINING 

The pretrained Wan VideoDiT predicts absolute future VAE latents, whereas our policy predicts future latents relative to the observed frame. Although the two representations contain the same information given that frame, they are different prediction targets. We therefore mid-train Wan before policy post-training to adapt its prediction space from absolute VAE latents to Residual-VAE latents. The known first frame carries persistent scene content, leaving the VideoDiT to predict changes such as robot motion, object displacement, and contact. Let E and D denote the frozen Wan VAE encoder and decoder. We encode the observed first frame and the real future clip, then broadcast the first-frame code across the future latent positions:

Here broadcast aligns the first-frame code with the shape of Zt+1: t+H . The anchor Bt is known at prediction time, only the residual Rt+1: t+H must be predicted. This change of coordinates loses no information relative to the original Wan latent:
