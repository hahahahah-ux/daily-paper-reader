RMMBench: A Comprehensive Benchmark
for Robotic Mobile Manipulation
Huapeng Li1, Fuxiang Feng1, Jinqiu Fan1, Shuo Yang1,
Fengjiao Chen2, Xuezhi Cao2, Ran Song1*, Wei Zhang1
1School of Control Science and Engineering, Shandong University
2Meituan Group
Abstract: Although the advancement of vision-language models (VLMs) has en-
dowed robots with enhanced environmental understanding and task reasoning, a
comprehensive evaluation methodology is important to advance the integration
of VLMs in robotic navigation and manipulation. However, current benchmarks
lack a comprehensive method to evaluate diverse robotic tasks, and evaluation
metrics remain relatively constrained, making it difficult to assess the embodied
capabilities of VLMs in a thorough and fine-grained manner. To address this is-
sue, we propose RMMBench, an evaluation benchmark that requires robots to
understand language instructions and perform long-horizon tasks in continuous
spaces. RMMBench seamlessly integrates high- and low-level embodied tasks
into a unified framework, constructing a “navigation-manipulation” task suite
comprising 70 canonical task scenarios that range from localized manipulation
to long-horizon composite navigation. The results reveal that leading VLMs still
face major challenges in spatial localization when performing mobile manipula-
tion tasks, and also highlight the necessity of enhancing the spatial perception ca-
pability of robots during long-horizon interactions. RMMBench can be accessed
at https://mxxq-stack.github.io/rmmbench-project/.
Keywords: Mobile Manipulation, Benchmark, VLM, Robot Learning

Introduction
The integration of natural language instructions to guide robotic navigation and manipulation tasks
represents an ongoing pursuit in the robotics community [1]. Traditional learning-based policies
and task and motion planning frameworks initially establish a classical pipeline that decouples high-
level reasoning from low-level execution [2, 3, 4, 5, 6]. Recently, large language models (LLMs) and
vision-language models (VLMs) have been widely adopted as high-level planners for robot learning
[7, 8, 9, 10]. This shift has led to the development of various benchmarks designed to evaluate these
models in robotic tasks.
Early benchmarks focused primarily on discrete symbolic tracking and high-level abstract task plan-
ning [11], limiting the output of a model to macro-commands or semantic goals. To test the pro-
ficiency of a model in handling diverse tasks, recent work introduced multi-stage, long-horizon
benchmarks featuring various scenarios [12, 13]. However, to isolate semantic reasoning or sim-
plify evaluation pipelines, such benchmarks commonly employed encapsulated interaction middle-
ware or separate vision components (such as SAM [14] or Grounding DINO [15]) to provide spatial
grounding [16, 17, 18].
Due to the inherent difficulty of alignment in navigation-to-manipulation transitions, existing bench-
marks for more complex mobile manipulation tasks typically relied on underlying navigation mod-
ules for path finding and resorted to heuristic-based grasping [17, 18]. However, this simplification
fails to rigorously test the autonomous navigation capability of the models and their precision in
execution. Consequently, they often relied on coarse-grained, outcome-oriented metrics (e.g., task
success rates) for performance evaluation, leaving the continuous tracking of intermediate action
execution unexamined. Most benchmarks characterized tasks through macro-level concepts such as
“semantic understanding” or “commonsense reasoning” [13, 16, 18]. Without fine-grained execu-
tion metrics, the actual quality of the generated low-level actions remains unquantified, failing to
provide detailed diagnostic insights. Therefore, a comprehensive benchmark based on raw visual
inputs and continuous execution is required to analyze the capability of a model in low-level spatial
reasoning and mobile manipulation.
To address such evaluation requirements and establish a more comprehensive diagnostic paradigm,
this work introduces RMMBench, an evaluation benchmark for grounded spatial perception and
composite mobile manipulation. Table 1 compares RMMBench with some popular benchmarks for
embodied intelligence. We construct a “navigation-manipulation” task suite comprising 70 canon-
ical task scenarios that range from localized manipulation to long-horizon composite navigation.
Within this suite, a robot must perform discrete, grid-based navigation toward target waypoints
and autonomously regulate its orientation for object alignment, while explicitly predicting pixel-
wise bounding boxes (BBoxes) to facilitate subsequent manipulation. To enable effective decision-
making in long-horizon tasks, RMMBench provides the complete textual history of past actions and
environmental feedback, while restricting the visual context to a sliding window of recent observa-
tions to prevent context explosion. Through this operational interface, we establish a fine-grained
evaluation protocol using 6 categories of process-oriented metrics to systematically analyze perfor-
mance across these composite tasks.
The composite tasks in our benchmark are designed to evaluate embodied models in sequential
navigation-manipulation coordination and long-horizon multi-goal planning. Unlike existing bench-
marks that rely on external navigation modules or predefined interaction triggers, we require models
to autonomously perform navigation, spatial alignment, and manipulation based solely on contin-
uous visual observations and environmental feedback. This setting imposes strict requirements on
low-level spatial grounding, temporal action consistency, and dynamic task scheduling in complex
environments. By explicitly modeling continuous execution processes and navigation-manipulation
transitions, RMMBench enables fine-grained diagnosis of grounded spatial reasoning and low-level
embodied decision-making beyond conventional task success metrics.

Related Work
Embodied Manipulation Benchmarks. The development of benchmarks and methodologies for
embodied manipulation has transitioned from discrete symbolic planning to semantic reasoning
driven by VLMs. Early long-horizon platforms [22, 23, 24] focused primarily on the construc-
tion of low-level skill chains, yet their task spaces were strictly limited to stationary tabletop or
cabinet-level operations. Although some room-scale environments [23, 25] extended the diversity
of scenarios, their underlying physical interactions often relied on discrete symbolic interfaces.
Modular Planning Paradigms. Embodied foundation models have diverged into end-to-end poli-
cies [26, 27, 28] and modular planning frameworks [1, 29]. Nevertheless, state-of-the-art modular
workflows [30] suffer from two key disconnections: one in perception and the other in interaction.
On the perception side, they suffer from “perceptual fragmentation” by relying heavily on exter-
nal, separate vision modules (e.g., SAM [14], Grounding DINO [15], or Set-of-Mark prompting
[11, 31, 32]) to heuristically extract BBoxes. Feeding these as symbolic labels to the foundation
model segregates semantic reasoning from native geometric spatial awareness. On the interaction
side, existing platforms mostly lack deep closed-loop testing for the “perception-execution discon-
nect”. When a VLM generates a faulty plan or encounters an unreachable state, prominent zero-shot
evaluation frameworks [29, 30] typically terminate the current episode or rely on open-loop implicit
corrections. To address these limitations, our work constructs a spatially extended “navigation-
manipulation” composite task hierarchy, requiring models to coordinate navigation between task
locations while maintaining native visual grounding during dynamic physical trial-and-error.
Procedural Evaluation Metrics. Although recent evaluation environments [13, 17] attempted to
introduce diversified evaluation criteria, current frameworks still tend to favor macro-level, outcome-
oriented metrics (such as final task success rates or coarse sub-task completion scores [11, 33, 34,
35, 36]) when quantifying subtle aspects such as redundant actions, wandering trajectories, or deci-
sion deadlocks. Such an evaluation bias easily obscures the procedural redundancy of agents dur-
ing long-term execution. To bridge this gap, we propose a fine-grained diagnostic framework that
achieves precise quantification of dynamic trial-and-error redundancy and subtle cognitive flaws in
large models.

RMMBench
RMMBench requires VLM to execute continuous-space, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks
based on language instructions and raw visual feedback. Evaluating this capability requires a tight
integration of sequential planning, spatial grounding, and closed-loop visual verification. In this
section, we present the benchmark design: Sec. 3.1 defines our multi-paradigm task hierarchy and
atomic skill libraries; Sec. 3.2 elaborates the comprehensive evaluation pipeline across navigation
and manipulation boundaries; and Sec. 3.3 establishes the comprehensive suite for fine-grained per-
formance evaluation.
3.1
Task Hierarchy and Atomic Skill Libraries
To evaluate the embodied perception, semantic reasoning, and zero-shot execution capabilities of
models, we construct a randomized data synthesis pipeline within a unified MuJoCo [37] physics
simulation environment supported by the dm control [38] framework, leveraging RoboCasa [12]
and Objaverse [39] assets. To improve physical plausibility, we apply geometric filtering to the
raw assets and exclude objects whose scale makes them unsuitable for grasping. For composite
navigation-manipulation tasks, the framework introduces diverse scenes from RoboCasa [12] across
multiple functional layouts and aesthetic styles. To evaluate policy robustness under distribution
shifts, each scenario dynamically randomizes target object categories, initial coordinates, lighting
conditions, and scene material textures during initialization.
RMMBench comprises 70 canonical task scenarios structured across two primary evaluation
paradigms to comprehensively stress-test VLMs. The first paradigm encompasses 60 standalone
manipulation scenarios, spanning from localized structured executions with deterministic routines
to open-ended interactions driven by high-level guidance. Instead of using rigid, template-matching
commands, we use diverse natural-language instructions that require common sense and complex
task-oriented reasoning, thereby forcing untuned VLMs to fully leverage their native world knowl-
edge for multi-step grounding and decision-making. Complementing these localized tasks, the sec-
ond paradigm introduces 10 composite navigation-manipulation scenarios that demand cross-space
skill chaining across multiple functional layouts.
The core atomic skill library exposes discrete primitives including pick, place, pull, lift, push, and
open door/close door, alongside active ego-perception via wrist observe. When a VLM selects a
skill, the DSL [40, 41] interface routes the command to a unified “RRT–IK–low-level controller
tracking” execution pipeline: the VLM is responsible only for high-level decisions, including skill
selection (with parameters), BBox prediction, and grasp candidate selection, while the backend
handles RRT planning, IK solving, and low-level control. Skills such as lift and pull use preset
default movement distances, and the VLM can increase the end-effector movement distance via
re-execution.

masks. For grasp execution, the options projected onto the wrist view comprise a hybrid candidate
pool containing both dynamically generated GraspNet predictions and expert-verified ground-truth
grasp annotations associated with each object asset. The latter serves as an environmental baseline
fallback to guarantee candidate availability across complex scenes. Specifically, the target pose for
pick is provided by the 6-DoF grasp pose generated by GraspNet, whereas the placement coordinates
for place are determined by the VLM-predicted BBox; grasp points are bound to object assets and
mapped to world coordinates via transformation matrices following the object’s actual pose. Cru-
cially, all pose candidates are anonymized and uniformly presented to the VLM. In tasks with con-
fined spaces or specific directional constraints (e.g., horizontal or vertical approach requirements),
GraspNet introduces necessary geometric directional diversity. The VLM must then autonomously
perform zero-shot spatial reasoning to select the optimal option for low-level point-to-point path
solvers.
Action History and Error Feedback. To support evaluations of long-horizon tasks, RMMBench
maintains the full textual history of past actions while restricting the visual context to a sliding win-
dow of recent frames. The simulator enforces strict physical reachability and kinematic constraints
without applying any implicit state corrections (e.g., automatic object teleportation or base reposi-

tioning). In most cases, the environment relies entirely on the subsequent sensory update, and the
VLM must autonomously verify the execution outcome, such as detecting an empty gripper or an
undisplaced target, solely through its visual observation stream. The only exception is text-based
feedback during pick and place execution, which is provided under two conditions: (1) the target
lies outside the reachable workspace; or (2) the predicted BBox contains no valid point cloud. All
other failures (e.g., collisions, slippage, anomalous object displacement) produce no textual hints
and expose no internal simulator state to the model. This design compensates for the model’s lack
of direct access to the robot’s kinematic constraints (e.g., arm length and reachable range), prevent-
ing the action budget from being exhausted on physically unreachable, ineffective operations. This
non-oracle feedback mechanism rigorously tests whether the model can detect execution anomalies
from raw vision and dynamically switch back to navigation or inspection primitives for repositioning
and replanning.
3.3
Evaluation Metrics
To provide a rigorous, non-binary evaluation of VLM performance in long-horizon composite tasks,
RMMBench provides a quantitative evaluation suite across three core dimensions: task execution
progress, visual grounding precision, and error-correction behavior. Specifically, task progression
and baseline efficiency are decomposed into execution efficiency (EE), two completion rates, and
a navigation progress measure. EE penalizes redundant actions against the expert reference se-
quence length, and is computed only over successful trajectories. The manipulation completion rate
(CRm) measures the proportion of satisfied atomic sub-conditions. The navigation completion rate
(CRn) monitors workspace radius and orientation alignment. The navigation progress (NP) tracks
spatial transit efficiency against the initial expert distance. To further evaluate high-level seman-
tic alignment, the expert action recall rate (RRact) [13] is formulated through the longest common
subsequence (LCS) relative to the length of the expert reference sequence:

4
Experiments
Our experimental design focuses on analyzing VLM behaviors across three core operational di-
mensions: (1) baseline task progression and commonsense planning performance under varying
task complexities; (2) decentralized navigation capability relying solely on first-person active vision
without global spatial priors; and (3) the stability and degradation patterns of 2D spatial grounding
under long-horizon execution.
4.1
Experimental Setups and Overall Performance
We evaluate five representative VLMs: Gemini-2.5-Pro [44], Gemini-3-Pro-preview, Gemini-3-
Flash-preview, Qwen-3.5-Plus, and Doubao-SEED-2.0-Pro-260215. Tests are conducted in a unified
MuJoCo simulation framework comprising 70 canonical task scenarios: 60 pure manipulation tasks
and 10 composite navigation-manipulation tasks. Domain randomization is applied across these
scenarios to support rigorous quantitative evaluation. Each task instance is evaluated over 3 inde-
pendent episodes to guarantee statistical reliability. Task configurations are pre-generated as fixed
JSON files, and all models load identical configurations to ensure fairness and reproducibility.
Experimental results demonstrate a clear decline in models’ performance as task horizon and com-
plexity increase.
While the models exhibit reasonable generalization in short-horizon, single-
interaction manipulation tasks, their success rates decrease when managing long-horizon tabletop
tasks that demand sequential coordination. The performance drops significantly when transitioning
further to composite navigation-manipulation tasks that require extended skill sequences. For these
long-horizon scenarios, the task success rates (SR) of the tested models fall to a low range of 10.0%
to 16.67%, exposing severe limitations in maintaining sustained execution stability.

However, for long-horizon manipulation tasks that require sequential action coordination, the overall
success rate decreases. Micro-level metric analysis reveals that this degradation is primarily driven
by localized execution failures. Specifically, when BBox accuracy drops and triggers an interaction
failure, the model repeats the exact same failed action primitive on the target entity instead of using
inspection primitives such as wrist camera observations to regain a clear field of view and replan the
execution. This operational pattern decreases RS, exposing a critical limitation in dynamic closed-
loop error correction and feedback-driven strategy adaptation during extended physical interactions.
Active Vision and Egocentric Navigation. We conduct an ablation study to compare navigation
performance with and without BEV mapping. The results listed in Tables 3 and 4 reveal a critical
bottleneck: stripping away global top-down priors leads to a severe degradation in both navigation
success rates and pathfinding efficiency across all models. Under purely egocentric constraints with
discrete movement and inspection action spaces, the model tends to rely heavily on rotation prim-
itives to keep the target object within its local field of view rather than proactively exploring the
environment via forward advancement, which drastically increases action redundancy.
Furthermore, due to the lack of global spatial references, a VLM tends to prematurely issue localized
manipulation commands before the mobile base reaches the workstation interaction radius. This
vulnerability is further exacerbated when encountering subtle obstacles. Despite having observed
these obstacles from a distance in previous frames, the model struggles to leverage historical visual
contexts for current obstacle avoidance. This highlights the fragility of egocentric spatial reasoning
and temporal visual memory in the VLM when deprived of external global coordinates.

of the predicted BBoxes over the course of execution to analyze how long-sequence inputs affect
the models. To disentangle the relationship between context length and localization accuracy, we
conduct a controlled experiment in which we fix the visual input, vary only the number of history
frames in the context (2–16), and evaluate the BBox IoU of each model under two conditions: a

5
Conclusion
We introduce RMMBench, a simulation benchmark designed to evaluate VLMs across grounded
spatial perception and composite mobile manipulation tasks. To augment conventional outcome-
oriented evaluation, RMMBench establishes a comprehensive evaluation suite to decouple planning
efficacy from localized execution failures. The experimental results reveal that although the state-of-
the-art VLMs possess adequate affordance knowledge for short-horizon manipulation, they exhibit
weak egocentric pathfinding when detached from global top-down priors, and their spatial localiza-
tion robustness under long sequences is model-dependent: models sensitive to complex visual condi-
tions show pronounced localization degradation as sequences accumulate. These findings highlight
critical bottlenecks in current modular VLM workflows and emphasize the necessity of enhancing
temporal visual memory and dynamic error correction for robust embodied deployment.
6
Limitations and Future Work
The current scope of RMMBench is limited by the number of canonical task scenarios and the gran-
ularity of the atomic skill library. The navigation pipeline uses a discretized grid abstraction. While
effective for isolating spatial reasoning, this setting simplifies the complexity of unstructured, real-
world terrain. Additionally, our manipulation pipeline employs a frozen, off-the-shelf GraspNet to
maintain generalization, which introduces localized grasping errors that impact task success. Fi-
nally, our controlled experiments reveal that the degradation of spatial grounding precision as the
context length grows is model-dependent: models such as Gemini-3-Flash remain stable or fluctuate
without a clear trend, whereas only SEED-2.0 exhibits a monotonic degradation, which is exac-
erbated under long-distance and occlusion conditions. Future work will focus on scaling the task
hierarchy, integrating head observation primitives to enhance environmental reactivity, fine-tuning
perception-grasping modules to mitigate localized errors, and conducting fine-grained experiments
to characterize the relationship between task horizon and grounding precision.
