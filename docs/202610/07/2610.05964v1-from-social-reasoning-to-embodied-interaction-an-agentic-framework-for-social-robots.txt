Abstract — Natural face-to-face human–robot interaction re-quires a robot to understand an evolving social situation, decide when to engage, and express its intent through co-ordinated physical behavior. Yet existing approaches rarely close this loop: foundation-model agents provide increasingly capable multimodal reasoning and memory but remain largely disembodied, while expressive virtual agents do not face the physical constraints of real robots, and physical social robots typically address social reasoning and embodied expression only partially. We present ARISE , a unified framework that bridges Agentic Reasoning and Interactive Social Embodiment on the Sophia humanoid robot. ARISE integrates multimodal context understanding, long-term memory, and reactive and proactive interaction to determine when and what to commu-nicate, and translates social intent into robot-native gestures coordinated with speech and mechanical facial expressions through streaming execution. Extensive evaluations on Sophia demonstrate strong perceived interaction quality, expressive and well-coordinated embodied behavior, and substantial la-tency reductions through streaming execution. These results highlight the importance of jointly reasoning about what to communicate, when to engage, and how to physically express social intent for natural interaction with humanoid robots. I. INTRODUCTION Empowering embodied agents with social intelligence is central to realizing general-purpose human–robot interaction. A capable social agent should not only infer human inten-tions from multi-modal interactions and generate appropriate responses, but also continuously perceive and reason about its surroundings, maintain awareness over extended interac-tions, and proactively engage with humans in real time [9], [5]. These capabilities become particularly compelling when embodied in humanoid robots, where perception, reasoning, communication, and physical behavior are tightly coupled in real-world interaction. Such physically grounded social intelligence therefore represents a promising direction toward interactive embodied agents that can naturally coexist and communicate with humans in everyday environments. Recent advances in foundation models have substantially expanded the capabilities of digital social agents, enabling natural, context-aware communication and long-horizon mul-timodal interaction [9]. Yet, such agents remain fundamen-tally disembodied, lacking the nonverbal behaviors essential to social interaction. Transferring these capabilities from digital embodiments to physical robots remains challenging: virtual motion is largely unconstrained, whereas physical execution must respect lim-ited degrees of freedom, joint limits, and hardware-specific actuation constraints. Physical social robots have begun to integrate foundation-model reasoning with expressive behav-ior, supporting affect-aware interaction [14], proactive assis-tance [5], interruption-aware dialogue [15], and expressive facial behavior [16], [6]. However, these capabilities have largely been studied in isolation. A unified physical social agent that can understand evolving social context, engage proactively, and coordinate verbal and nonverbal behavior remains largely unexplored. To address this gap, we present a unified embodied social interaction system deployed on the Sophia [17] humanoid robot. At its core, a multimodal social agent directly rea-sons over conversational audio and visual observations, aug-mented with long-term memory, online knowledge retrieval, and proactive interaction to support both reactive and self-initiated engagement. An embodiment-aware gesture planner further translates response intent into verified symbolic ges-ture programs composed of executable upper-body motion primitives. To realize these high-level decisions as coherent physical behavior, we introduce a streaming multimodal orchestration scheme that synchronizes speech with audio-driven facial animation and schedules gestures online using the audio–facial stream as a shared temporal reference. To-gether, these components couple multimodal social reasoning with coordinated verbal and nonverbal behavior, enabling context-aware and proactive face-to-face interaction on a physical humanoid robot. We summarize our core contributions as follows: 1) We present ARISE , a unified framework for physical humanoid social interaction that combines multimodal social reasoning, long-term memory, and coordinated em-bodied expression, supporting both reactive and proactive engagement. 2) We bridge high-level agentic social reasoning and low-level embodiment-aware gesture planning to translate so-cial intent into executable robot behavior. Streaming Mul-timodal Orchestration further coordinates asynchronous speech, facial animation, and gesture streams for progres-sive embodied execution. 3) Extensive evaluations on Sophia demonstrate strong context-aware social interaction, effective proactive en-gagement, and expressive, coordinated embodied behav-ior. II. RELATED WORKS 

A. Social Reasoning with Foundation Models 

Large models have expanded social interaction from dia-logue generation toward multimodal understanding, memory, and contextual planning [18]. Socratic Models [19] and embodied multimodal models such as PaLM-E [20] compose pretrained models through language to support multimodal reasoning and assistive dialogue. Generative Agents [21] in-tegrate memory, reflection, and planning to produce sustained social behavior in simulated environments. M3-Agent [9] fur-ther extends memory-based reasoning to visual and auditory experience through episodic and semantic memory. Together, these approaches provide increasingly capable foundations for understanding social context and formulating appropriate responses. However, social reasoning alone does not deliver the physical presence and expressive behavior needed for immersive interaction. Our framework bridges this gap by translating multimodal, memory-augmented reasoning into coordinated speech, facial expressions, and upper-body ges-tures on a physical humanoid. 

B. Co-speech Gesture Generation 

Co-speech gesture generation has advanced toward expres-sive, holistic animation of 3D digital humans. EMAGE [1] integrates audio conditioning with masked gesture model-ing to generate coordinated face, body, and hand motion. DiffSHEG [22] jointly models facial expressions and body gestures through diffusion, supporting synchronized mo-tion generation for arbitrary-length speech. More recently, MIBURI [3] enables online, causal generation of expressive gestures and facial expressions alongside streaming dialogue. These methods advance motion quality and responsive-ness primarily in virtual human representations. However, transferring such expressiveness to physical robots requires accommodating fewer degrees of freedom and hardware-specific joint limits. We address this challenge by compos-ing and verifying robot-native gesture programs to support semantic expressiveness and speech coordination within the robot’s constrained motion repertoire. 

C. Multimodal Interaction in Social Robotics 

Social robotics has progressed from coordinated multi-modal expression toward adaptive, reasoning-driven interac-tion. Early work by Aly and Tapus [4] combines emotional speech, facial expressions, and speech-accompanying head and arm gestures in narrative interaction. More recent sys-tems advance social decision-making: Attentive Support [5] reasons about when and how to assist human groups, while Garello et al. [7] integrate LLM-based interaction manage-ment with knowledge-graph memory for adaptive tutoring. In parallel, speech-driven animatronic control and ExFace improve expressive facial actuation on physical robots [16], [6]. ProAct [8] further connects memory-based proactive reasoning with streaming humanoid motion, bringing social planning and embodied behavior closer together. However, these systems typically emphasize selected as-pects of social reasoning or embodied expression, leaving their integration incomplete, as summarized in Table I. Our framework unifies memory-augmented reactive and proactive planning with coordinated speech, mechanical facial expres-sions, and verified upper-body gestures through a shared streaming pipeline, enabling context-aware, expressive, and responsive interaction on a physical humanoid. III. M ETHOD 

A. Overview 

As illustrated in Fig. 2, our framework maps ongoing audio–visual interaction to coordinated verbal and nonverbal behaviors on the Sophia [17] human-like robot. Given con-versational audio At and RGB observation Vt , the Multimodal Social Agent generates a verbal response and an executable symbolic gesture plan using multimodal reasoning, long-term memory, online retrieval, and proactive interaction. The Streaming Multimodal Orchestration module converts these high-level outputs into synchronized speech, facial animation, and temporally scheduled gestures. The Robot Deployment module finally maps the coordinated signals to Sophia’s actuators. This design separates semantic social planning from temporal coordination and low-level execu-tion.

C. Streaming Multimodal Orchestration 

This module converts Rt and ˆ St into a coordinated stream-ing response. It first synchronizes speech and facial anima-tion, then uses the audio–facial stream as the reference clock for gesture scheduling. The generated speech is streamed as temporally ordered audio chunks at,r. Each chunk is sent in parallel to the audio buffer and Audio2Face, which produces the corresponding facial blendshape chunk

D. Robot Deployment 

Deploying ARISE on Sophia requires converting hetero-geneous behavioral representations into robot-specific com-mands while preserving their temporal structure. Speech arrives as audio chunks, facial motion as blendshape se-quences, and upper-body behavior as symbolic keyframes, each requiring a different physical realization. Audio is dispatched to the loudspeaker, while Audio2Face outputs are retargeted through ExFace [6] to Sophia’s mechanical facial actuators. Each gesture keyframe is resolved through a pre-defined mapping to a validated upper-body motor target. This representation-specific translation grounds behavior in the robot’s available actuation space without exposing hardware-level control parameters to the language-based planner. At runtime, the deployment layer dispatches mapped com-mands according to the shared audio–facial clock established by Streaming Multimodal Orchestration. Audio playback and facial actuation follow the paired chunk sequence, while upper-body motor targets are issued according to their as-signed intervals It,m, preserving the planned keyframe order and durations across modality-specific interfaces. IV. EXPERIMENTS To validate the performance of the framework and assess its practical applicability to embodied human–robot interac-tion, we investigate the following research questions: 

Q1. How effectively does the framework support sustained and context-aware multimodal social interaction? 

Q2. How effectively does the framework generate expressive robot behaviors that are semantically and temporally aligned with the interaction content? 

Q3. How effectively do the proposed engineering strategies mitigate latency accumulation across cascaded multimodal processing? 

A. Experimental Setup 

Hardware Setup. We conduct our experiments on the Sophia [17] humanoid robot developed by Hanson Robotics. An Intel RealSense RGB camera is mounted between Sophia’s head and chest to capture visual observations, and a Philips DLM3545C Bluetooth microphone captures speech. Our framework runs on a host computer equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, which communicates with Sophia through the robot’s native control interface to deliver speech, facial expressions, and upper-body gestures. 

Evaluation Metrics. All ratings were collected using a five-point Likert scale, where 1 denotes “strongly disagree” and 5 denotes “strongly agree.” We used different rating criteria for the two user studies. For the Multi-round Interaction experiment, participants evaluated each interaction session using four criteria:  

> •

Overall Interaction Quality (OIQ): To what extent do the robot’s verbal and non-verbal behaviors together form a coherent and satisfactory social interaction?  

> •

Contextual Appropriateness (CA): To what extent is the robot’s response appropriate given the user’s utterance, visible environment, and current interaction context?  

> •

Conversational Continuity (CC): To what extent does the robot’s response remain consistent with information and events established earlier in the interaction?  

> •

Interaction Engagement (IE): To what extent did the interaction maintain your interest and attention? For the Gesture Generation experiment, participants eval-uated each interaction session using four criteria:  

> •

Motion Naturalness : To what extent do the robot’s movements appear smooth, physically plausible, and natural?  

> •

Motion Expressiveness : To what extent do the robot’s movements convey the intended social or communica-tive meaning?  

> •

Speech-Motion Coordination : To what extent are the robot’s movements temporally and semantically coor-dinated with its speech?  

> •

Overall Quality : Considering both the robot’s speech and movements, how would you rate the overall quality of this response? 

User Study. We recruited 20 participants who had no prior knowledge of our system for each of Q1 and Q2. Each par-ticipant completed the full evaluation for all configurations within the corresponding experiment. For Q1, participants were provided with a predefined dia-logue script to ensure comparable interaction content, while retaining the freedom to ask natural follow-up questions based on the robot’s responses. For Q2, participants were provided with predefined dialogue scripts and were asked to follow the scripts while focusing on the robot’s verbal and gestural responses. After each interaction, participants rated the robot accord-ing to the evaluation metrics associated with the correspond-ing study. The order of configurations was independently ran-domized for each participant. Participants were not allowed to see or interact with the robot before the experiment, and not informed of the configuration used in each interaction or whether it corresponded to the baseline or an ablation. Before each interaction, the robot was reset to the same predefined initial state. 

Statistical Analysis. For each configuration, mean and standard deviation were computed across the 20 participant ratings. We applied two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests [26] to participant-level scores. P-values were adjusted using the Holm procedure [27]. Significance was denoted by  

> ∗

for Padj < 0.05, ∗∗ for Padj < 0.01, and ∗∗∗ for Padj < 0.001. 

B. Baselines and Ablation 

To assess the contributions of individual components to interaction-response and embodied-gesture quality, we eval-uated the Full System together with the following controlled ablation settings. For Q1, we compare four configurations: Full System ,which enables visual input, long-term memory, and proactive interaction; w/o Visual Input , which removes the current RGB observation; w/o Long-Term Memory , which disables memory retrieval and update; and w/o Proactive Interac-tion , which disables proactive behaviors while retaining user-initiated response generation. For Q2, we compare four configurations of our 

Embodiment-Aware Gesture Generation framework. Full System denotes the complete configuration. Single Candi-date changes the number of generated candidates from J = 3to J = 1. w/o Review-and-Refinement Agent removes the review-and-refinement stage and directly generates and exe-cutes a single gesture sequence. We also include EMAGE [1] as an external speech-driven gesture baseline. EMAGE gen-erates human-body motion in the SMPL-X representation. We retain only its upper-body motion and project it into Sophia’s joint space. Since Sophia’s joints are modeled as single-degree-of-freedom actuators, this projection reduces the corresponding SMPL-X motion to one-dimensional joint commands. Before execution, a safety check clips any com-mand that exceeds the allowable joint range of the robot.

The current cascaded architecture still limits sub-second conversational responsiveness, with most latency arising from multimodal-agent inference and streaming facial syn-thesis rather than robot execution. Fine-grained facial ex-pressiveness also remains limited on the current hardware platform. Mechanical facial actuation constrains the sub-tle motions that can be physically realized, intro- ducing platform-dependent limits distinct from the proposed social planning and orchestration mechanisms. Future work will explore lightweight models specialized for social interaction and robot-native facial synthesis to further reduce response latency and improve embodied responsiveness. VI. C ONCLUSION 

We presented ARISE , a unified framework that connects multimodal social reasoning with coordinated embodied in-teraction on the Sophia humanoid. By integrating memory-augmented reactive and proactive interaction, robot-native gesture planning, and streaming speech–face–gesture orches-tration, ARISE improves perceived interaction and gesture quality while reducing latency relative to serial embodiment processing.
