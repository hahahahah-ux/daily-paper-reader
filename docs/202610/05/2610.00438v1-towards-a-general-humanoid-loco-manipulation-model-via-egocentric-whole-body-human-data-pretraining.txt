ABSTRACT
Humanoid whole-body manipulation has advanced rapidly, enabling policies to
coordinate locomotion, posture, bimanual interaction, and dexterous hand move-
ments. Meanwhile, egocentric human videos provide diverse examples of every-
day interactions across objects and scenes, offering scalable supervision without
robot operation. However, existing supervision from these videos provides lim-
ited coverage of whole-body movement and coordination with hand–object inter-
action, while obtaining such supervision through humanoid teleoperation is also
costly and difficult to scale. We therefore explore how human experience can
support scalable learning of humanoid loco-manipulation. To support this study,
we introduce HUMANVERSE-500, a 500-hour dataset of diverse human loco-
manipulation behaviors in open-world environments, collected with a lightweight
wearable system that synchronizes egocentric video with body and hand mo-
tion. Building on this dataset, we develop λ0, a whole-body humanoid vision–
language–action policy, through three-stage training that first learns interaction
from diverse egocentric datasets, then coordinates body and hand motion using
HUMANVERSE-500, and finally adapts the policy to downstream tasks and robot
embodiments. Across these stages, λ0 learns a shared representation space for
human experience transfer, while domain-specific interfaces handle differences
between human and robot states and actions. We evaluate λ0 on SIMPLE and
4 real-world loco-manipulation tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance, and
further analyze its scaling behavior, generalization, and training-stage contribu-
tions to understand how human data support downstream whole-body humanoid
control. We will release our code, models, and data to support further research.

INTRODUCTION
Humanoid whole-body manipulation has advanced rapidly, enabling robots to coordinate locomo-
tion, posture, and dexterous manipulation within a unified policy (Jiang et al., 2026; Shi et al., 2026;
Wei et al., 2026b; Hu et al., 2026; Figure AI, 2026a;b). Learning these coordinated behaviors re-
quires demonstrations that capture both whole-body movement and interaction with objects. Such
demonstrations can be collected through whole-body teleoperation, where human operators guide
humanoids through coordinated movements and object interactions (Fu et al., 2025a; He et al., 2025;
Luo et al., 2026b; GEAR Team et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026b). Collecting diverse demonstrations this
way requires substantial robot time and human effort, making it difficult to scale across tasks and
environments (Shi et al., 2026; Punamiya et al., 2026).
Egocentric human videos offer a scalable alternative, capturing diverse everyday interactions with-
out requiring robot operation (Grauman et al., 2022; Kareer et al., 2025; Hoque et al., 2026;
Punamiya et al., 2026; Figure, 2026). Recent methods recover wrist and hand trajectories from these
videos, turning observed interactions into action supervision for robot learning (Li et al., 2026a;
Yang et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). Pretraining on these action-labeled videos can improve down-
stream manipulation, supporting the use of human experience for scalable robot learning (Zheng
et al., 2026b; Punamiya et al., 2026). However, wrist and hand trajectories alone do not fully capture
the whole-body coordination required for humanoid loco-manipulation. A humanoid must approach
an object, adjust its posture to reach it, and maintain its grasp while carrying it elsewhere (Shi et al.,
2026; Zhao et al., 2026a). Learning these behaviors requires supervision that preserves the temporal
coordination between body movement and hand–object interaction. We therefore ask how diverse
human experience can provide scalable whole-body supervision for humanoid loco-manipulation.
To address this question, we introduce HUMANVERSE-500, a 500-hour dataset of human loco-
manipulation collected across diverse activities in open-world environments. We use a lightweight
wearable system to capture egocentric video and body motion, then reconstruct hand motion from
video to provide synchronized whole-body supervision. Building on HUMANVERSE-500, we de-
velop λ0, a whole-body humanoid vision–language–action policy that transfers human experience to
robot control (Fig. 1). We train the policy in three stages to learn interaction from diverse egocentric
datasets, coordinate body and hand motion using HUMANVERSE-500, and adapt to downstream
tasks and robot embodiments. A shared representation space enables transfer across stages, while
domain-specific interfaces handle differences between human and robot states and actions.
We evaluate λ0 on SIMPLE and four real-world loco-manipulation tasks, improving success rates
over the strongest evaluated baselines by 3.9 and 22.5 percentage points, respectively. Across the
evaluated data scales, more whole-body human data reduces validation loss and improves real-world
task progress. Ablations further identify whole-body mid-training as the larger contributor to real-
world success among the two human-data stages, while human demonstrations improve progress
on robot-unseen objects. These results support scalable whole-body human data as a source of
supervision for humanoid loco-manipulation. Our contributions are summarised as follows:
• We introduce HUMANVERSE-500, a 500-hour dataset of open-world human loco-
manipulation with synchronized egocentric video, body motion, and hand motion.
• We develop λ0, a whole-body humanoid vision–language–action policy that transfers hu-
man experience through three-stage training in a shared human–robot representation space.
• We reveal a consistent scaling trend in whole-body human pretraining and demonstrate its
benefits for downstream performance and generalization in humanoid loco-manipulation.

RELATED WORK
2.1
GENERALIST ROBOT POLICIES
Large-scale robot datasets bring together demonstrations across tasks and embodiments, supporting
the development of generalist policies (Dasari et al., 2020; Jang et al., 2022; Walke et al., 2023;
Open X-Embodiment Collaboration, 2024; Brohan et al., 2023; Zitkovich et al., 2023; Khazatsky
et al., 2024; Bu et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026b). These policies learn from diverse demonstrations
within a shared model (Ghosh et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2025; Chi et al., 2025), motivating more scal-

able ways to collect training data. Advances in teleoperation and portable demonstration interfaces
reduce collection effort (Dass et al., 2023; Qin et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2023; Fu et al., 2025b; Chi
et al., 2024). However, robot-based collection remains tied to hardware availability and repeated
environment resets, while portable interfaces capture motion within their supported action spaces.
2.2
LEARNING FROM HUMAN VIDEOS
Human video datasets capture diverse activities, objects, and environments, providing rich expe-
rience for robot learning (Grauman et al., 2022; Damen et al., 2022; Goyal et al., 2017; Grauman
et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023b; DeTone et al., 2026; Lightwheel AI, 2026). Learning from it requires
connecting observations to actions. Temporal and geometric annotations describe when interactions
occur and how hands and objects move (Liu et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2025; Sener et al., 2022; Ra-
gusa et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2024; Banerjee et al., 2025), while motion tracking and reconstruction
provide human trajectories for action learning (Hoque et al., 2026; Punamiya et al., 2026; Li et al.,
2026a; Zheng et al., 2026b; Wang et al., 2026). Complementary approaches learn visual represen-
tations from human videos (Nair et al., 2023; Sontakke et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2020) or connect
human demonstrations to robot control through imitation and alignment (Mandlekar et al., 2022;
Shao et al., 2020; Bahl et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023a; Bharadhwaj et al., 2023; Kareer et al., 2025;
Lepert et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2026). Human videos offer scalable supervision for manipulation,
but capturing whole-body coordination remains challenging for humanoid learning.
2.3
HUMANOID WHOLE-BODY CONTROL
Humanoid whole-body control coordinates locomotion, posture, and manipulation to support com-
plex physical interactions (Fu et al., 2025a; He et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2026b). Building on this con-
trol foundation, humanoid policies learn task-level behaviors from human experience through latent
video actions, geometric pretraining, or joint training on human and robot demonstrations (Jiang
et al., 2026; Wei et al., 2026b; Shi et al., 2026; Hu et al., 2026; Bjorck et al., 2025; Luo et al.,
2026a). Predictive models and integrated whole-body systems offer complementary ways to con-
nect perception, motion, and control (Li et al., 2026b; Symbiosis Robotics, 2026; Figure AI, 2026a;
Parada, 2026; Zheng et al., 2026a; Ye et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026). A remaining challenge is to
turn diverse human experience into scalable supervision for humanoid whole-body control.

DATA COVERAGE

3.2
WHOLE-BODY MOTION RECONSTRUCTION
We construct whole-body motion by combining wearable-tracked body kinematics with hand motion
reconstructed from video. The PICO system (PICO, n.d.) provides timestamped 24-joint body
motion, while Dyn-HaMR (Yu et al., 2025) reconstructs 21 joints per hand using camera motion
estimated by ViPE (Huang et al., 2025). Timestamps and coordinate transforms align body and
hand trajectories with video frames, while an episode-fixed frame preserves hand displacement.
Validity masks retain usable body supervision when hands are missing or occluded. Quality checks
cover timestamp consistency, finite values, hand reprojections, and synchronized motion overlays.

λ0: LEARNING HUMANOID CONTROL FROM HUMAN EXPERIENCE
We develop λ0 with a shared vision–language–action architecture and domain-specific interfaces to
transfer human experience to humanoid control. Training proceeds through interaction pre-training,
whole-body mid-training, and embodiment-specific post-training (Fig. 6).
4.1
POLICY ARCHITECTURE AND ACTION INTERFACES
Given an egocentric image x, a language instruction ℓ, and the current state sq, λ0 predicts a 50-step
action chunk in the active action space q. The backbone and action expert are shared across stages,
while domain-specific action projections handle differences in target dimensions and semantics.
Shared transfer core.
λ0 consists of a Qwen3.5-2B (Qwen Team, 2026) vision–language back-
bone Vϕ and a generative action expert Fψ. The current state is quantized into 256 bins and appended
to the language instruction. The backbone encodes this state-aware instruction together with the im-
age x, then supplies layer-wise features to the action expert. For each action space q, an input
encoder Eq maps the noisy action and noise level into action tokens, while an output decoder Dq
maps the shared features to a velocity prediction in that action space:
bvq = Dq(Fψ(Vϕ(x, [ℓ; Q(sq)]), Eq(eaq, σ), e)) .
(1)
Here e is the embodiment identifier. The backbone Vϕ and action expert Fψ are shared across stages,
while separate input encoders Eq and output decoders Dq handle the corresponding action spaces.
Action interfaces.
The egocentric interface predicts hand motion conditioned on video, language,
and bilateral hand states. The robot-compatible view of mid-training data uses the same state–action
format as downstream G1 post-training data, while the two stages use distinct training domains.

training on egocentric videos and hand poses, HUMANVERSE-500 (DW ) for mid-training on whole-

All stages use the masked velocity-field loss Lq defined in Sec. 4.3, with different data sources and
active interfaces. Let θ(k) denote the policy parameters learned in Stage k. Stages II and III initialize
from the preceding EMA checkpoint while changing the supervision and active interfaces.

EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate whether the three-stage recipe turns human data into improved humanoid control and
object transfer. Specifically, we ask: Q1. How well does the model perform on humanoid loco-
manipulation? (Sec. 5.2); Q2. How do human-data scale and the training recipe affect learning?
(Sec. 5.3); Q3. Does human-data scaling improve downstream transfer? (Sec. 5.4). Accordingly,
we evaluate held-out learning dynamics, task performance, and unseen-object generalization.
5.1
EVALUATION SETUP AND METRICS
Figure 7: Real-world data collection and evaluation. (a) Egocentric teleoperation collects whole-

Hardware. As shown in Fig. 7(a), a PICO 4U with five trackers captures body motion, which

tasks. Standard trials use robot-seen objects in Fig. 7(c). Generalization uses robot-unseen objects

Baselines. Simulation baselines come from the public SIMPLE leaderboard (Wei et al., 2026a).

CONCLUSION
We presented λ0, a whole-body humanoid vision–language–action policy that transfers human ex-
perience through three-stage training. We introduced HUMANVERSE-500, a 500-hour dataset of di-
verse open-world human activities with synchronized egocentric video, body motion, and hand mo-
tion. Experiments on SIMPLE and four real-world tasks demonstrated improved loco-manipulation
performance and transfer to robot-unseen objects. Controlled experiments revealed consistent ben-
efits from scaling whole-body human data and highlighted the contribution of whole-body mid-
training. These findings support scalable human activity as rich supervision for humanoid control.
Limitations and future work.
Our evaluation focuses on G1, leaving transfer to full-sized hu-
manoids untested. Despite camera alignment and motion retargeting, differences in body propor-
tions, viewpoints, and feasible motions leave human–robot alignment imperfect. Future work will
extend λ0 to full-sized humanoids through improved motion retargeting and closer alignment be-
tween egocentric observations, whole-body trajectories, and physically executable robot actions.
