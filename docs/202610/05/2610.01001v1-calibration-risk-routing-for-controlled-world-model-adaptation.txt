## ABSTRACT 

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) can exploit simulated experience, but a simulator-to-target shift creates a model-selection problem: correcting the simulator and fitting the target directly can each fail under limited target data. We introduce the Model-Corrected World Model (MC-WM), which separates initial target data into disjoint fit, selection, and calibration partitions and deploys the family with lower standardized calibration risk. A learned confidence signal and deterministic validity predicates weight one-step imagined policy updates without rewriting physical rewards. We evaluate 540 unique reported run cells across three controlled Multi-Joint dynamics with Contact (MuJoCo) shifts; one exact-routing cell was repeated after a pre-deployment artifact gate, giving 541 completed executions. In a fresh ten-seed confirmation, MC-WM exceeds target Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) return by 1925 .7 [715 .6, 3322 .3], 1184 .5 [724 .5, 1752 .7], and 133 .4 [67 .6, 197 .9] on Ant actuator, HalfCheetah gravity, and Walker friction, respectively, while reducing violations in all three. It also exceeds matched Domain Adaptation with Rewards from Classifiers (DARC) return in every environment, but its return differences from direct target Model-Based Policy Optimization (MBPO) remain unresolved. The selector chooses direct target models for all Ant and gravity runs and residual models for all Walker runs. Automatic routing exactly reproduces its selected forced arm in all 30 paired checks and improves Ant return over the unselected arm by 1529 .9 [426 .9, 2833 .1]; gravity and Walker return and all route-level violation differences remain unresolved. A separate sparse-versus-dense study finds fewer residual parameters but no resolved endpoint advantage. The evidence supports calibrated model-family choice and bounded trust mechanisms, not universal residual or symbolic superiority. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Simulation can supply control data without exposing a target system to every exploratory action. Dynamics randomization and rapid adaptation use this advantage to train reinforcement learning (RL) policies that tolerate or identify dynamics variation (Peng et al., 2017; Kumar et al., 2021). MBRL instead fits a predictive model and improves the policy with imagined transitions. Under a simulator-to-target dynamics shift, however, either reusing the simulator model or fitting a target model from limited data can fail. Policy optimization can then exploit errors that were rare under the model-fitting distribution. Probabilistic ensembles represent model uncertainty (Chua et al., 2018), and short branched rollouts limit compounding error (Janner et al., 2019), but neither decides whether simulator correction or direct target modeling is better for a particular shift. Model fitting must also account for the state-action distribution induced by policy learning (Ross & Bagnell, 2012). This leaves two coupled questions: which target-model family should be deployed, and how strongly should each imagined transition influence policy learning? We study these questions in MC-WM. MC-WM fits two candidates from the same initial target data: a residual correction to a frozen simulator ensemble and a direct target ensemble. A disjoint calibration partition measures standardized joint state-and-reward error and fixes the lower-risk family 1arXiv:2610.01001v1 [cs.AI] 1 Oct 2026 for the run. Direct-candidate fitting uses an isolated random stream, so model-family selection cannot change downstream policy or replay initialization merely by consuming random numbers. The deployed model then produces one-step imagined transitions whose policy-critic losses are weighted by a learned confidence signal and deterministic validity predicates. On the residual route, the state correction combines a locked sparse component with a masked neural remainder; the support, coefficients, calibration errors, routing decision, filter behavior, and target access are exported for every run. The paper makes three contributions. First, it defines calibration-risk routing between residual and direct target world models using disjoint fit, selection, and calibration partitions. Second, it provides an exact routing counterfactual: automatic, forced-residual, and forced-direct arms fit identical candidates under matched random streams, and the automatic arm must exactly reproduce its selected forced arm. Third, it evaluates the complete method and its limits through 540 unique reported run cells from 541 completed executions: a fresh ten-seed main comparison on three controlled dynamics shifts and morphologies, the routing counterfactual, a source-identical predecessor mechanism matrix, and a matched sparse-versus-dense residual study. The evidence is deliberately bounded to target MuJoCo simulators. It does not imply physical sim-to-real transfer, stable symbolic discovery across seeds, reduced total target access, or a safety guarantee. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Adaptation across dynamics. Visual domain randomization trains perception networks across randomized rendering conditions (Tobin et al., 2017); dynamics randomization trains control policies across randomized physics (Peng et al., 2017). Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) infers an environment representation online (Kumar et al., 2021). DARC estimates a source-to-target reward correction from state-action and transition domain classifiers (Eysenbach et al., 2020). Hybrid Offline-and-Online RL with dynamics gaps (H2O+) combines limited offline target data with online simulator interaction (Niu et al., 2023). MC-WM differs by fitting explicit residual and direct target world-model candidates, selecting between them on held-out transition risk, and weighting model-generated policy updates. DARC is included under the same target-training and held-out evaluation contract as the other main methods. Residual world-model adaptation is the closest model class. Lanier et al. (2025) adapt visual latent world models using offline, reward-unlabelled target data and latent-state dynamics residuals. MC-WM uses reward-labelled online target data and observation-space increments, places a locked sparse component and masked neural remainder on the residual route, and allows calibration risk to reject that route in favor of a direct target ensemble. These are architectural and protocol differences; they do not establish dominance across the distinct visual-control and state-control settings. 

World models and policy optimization. Probabilistic Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PETS) represents predictive uncertainty with probabilistic dynamics ensembles (Chua et al., 2018). MBPO limits model bias with short rollouts branched from real data (Janner et al., 2019). Dreamer learns behaviors through latent imagination (Hafner et al., 2019). Temporal Difference Learning for Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC) combines latent dynamics with a terminal value estimate for planning (Hansen et al., 2022). MC-WM uses one-step rollouts so that the experiment primarily tests target-model choice, correction, and weighting rather than long-horizon compounding. 

Structured residuals and constrained updates. Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics (SINDy) estimates sparse dynamics from a prescribed feature library (Brunton et al., 2015). MC-WM applies its sparse-regression principle to target-minus-simulator state increments, but the sparse branch is evaluated as a capacity and inspectability choice rather than assumed to improve control. Policy learning is based on SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018). The project-specific three-critic pessimistic actor is related to ensemble methods such as Randomized Ensembled Double Q-Learning (REDQ), but is not a REDQ implementation (Chen et al., 2021). Constrained Policy Optimization optimizes an expected-cost constraint (Achiam et al., 2017), whereas Inverse Constrained Reinforcement Learning infers constraints from demonstrations (Anwar et al., 2020). The Gravity and Friction Walker thresholds apply continuous quadratic reward penalties, whereas the Ant environment combines a quadratic velocity penalty with termination when the world-frame base position is below −3 along the first horizontal coordinate. The deterministic predicate set used by MC-WM does not contain an Ant-specific predicate in the reported protocol; Ant violations remain environment outcomes and training costs rather than predicate-filter events. 4.2 CANDIDATE FREEZING AND MAIN COMPARATORS 

The final candidate was fixed before the confirmation endpoints were read. A registered seven-seed development screen replaced the predecessor’s indirect state-error-gap route with the calibration risk in Equation (3). A subsequent implementation audit found that fitting the direct candidate had consumed the global random stream before policy initialization. We corrected this causal confound by isolating the component random stream as described in Section 3.2, froze the corrected source, and discarded the affected confirmation endpoints from the submission evidence. The final ten-seed bank was then sampled separately. It excludes all 40 seeds registered by the preceding development and confirmation studies. Development outcomes are used only for algorithm selection; all main performance claims use the fresh, random-stream-isolated confirmation matrix. The main matrix contains the frozen calibration-routed MC-WM candidate in Algorithm 1 and five controls. Residual MC-WM core retains the sparse-plus-neural residual, confidence critic, and policy-aware refitting, but fixes residual deployment and removes adaptive update routing, the violation cost, and validation-based checkpoint restoration. Direct target MBPO fits the same probabilistic world-model architecture directly to target transitions and uses one-step rollouts. Target SAC learns from target transitions without a learned world model. Our matched-protocol DARC implementation uses the published additive state-action and state-action-next-state classifiers, 32-unit hidden layers, unit Gaussian input noise, and starts reward correction after 4,000 online updates (Eysenbach et al., 2020). The warmup is scaled to our 40,000-step online phase and is not presented as a reproduction of DARC’s original longer schedule. Raw simulator SAC learns for 50,000 interactions in the uncorrected simulator and receives no target-domain training transition. All SAC-labelled methods use the same project-specific policy backbone described in Section 3.5; “SAC” does not denote an otherwise unchanged reference implementation. The primary comparisons are candidate versus residual MC-WM core and direct target MBPO. They test whether calibrated family selection improves over a fixed residual and whether simulator correction improves over direct target modeling. Target SAC, DARC, and raw simulator SAC are secondary controls for model-free target learning, classifier-based adaptation, and zero-target-training transfer, respectively. Every method is run for ten independently trained seeds on all three environments, giving 3 × 6 × 10 = 180 confirmation runs from one frozen source tree. 64.3 TRAINING AND EVALUATION ACCESS

## 6 DISCUSSION 

The strongest result is model-family choice rather than residual modeling alone. Calibration risk routes Ant and gravity to direct target models and Walker to residual models. Exact selected-arm equality rules out an independent override effect; Ant shows a return benefit with no resolved route harm elsewhere. The main matrix also favors MC-WM over target SAC and matched DARC, but return relative to direct target MBPO remains unresolved. The supported claim is therefore calibrated choice under these shifts, not universal routing or model-based dominance. The predecessor matrix supports checkpoint restoration, Ant fixed-route avoidance, and a violation-cost tradeoff, but not separate universal effects of confidence or predicates. Sparse residuals use fewer parameters and export per-run equations, yet show no endpoint advantage, mixed calibration error, and low cross-seed support overlap. Inspectability holds; stable structural discovery and sparse control superiority do not. 6.1 LIMITATIONS 

The evidence covers three controlled MuJoCo shifts, not physical sim-to-real transfer or within-task route switching. DARC is the only external adaptation method under the identical access contract. Every adaptive method receives 50,000 target training interactions plus validation and test access, and the candidate may use validation for actor restoration; reduced total access and one-shot adaptation are not established. Confidence and predicates are empirical weights, not uncertainty bounds or safety guarantees. The Ant observation omits absolute planar position, although its radial reward and wall termination depend on that position. Its observation-space MDP is therefore an approximation: identical model inputs can have different rewards or wall outcomes, limiting the reward and termination heads. E.2 ACTOR ROUTING , CHECKPOINT SELECTION , AND COMPLETE PROCEDURE 

The initial state-only calibration ratio ϱcal divides the frozen simulator state RMSE by the residual candidate state RMSE on Ical . When 2 ≤ ϱcal < 4.5, actor updates use equal numbers of target and imagined states; imagined actor samples carry weight (wi)1/2, and the actor updates after every four critic updates. Outside this band, target and imagined replay use the standard separate SAC updates. At each 5,000-step checkpoint, five fixed validation episodes yield mean undiscounted return Gu and mean violations per episode V u. MC-WM tracks the highest-return actor satisfying V u ≤ 1. Before the one-time test, that actor replaces the final actor only if either (i) final validation return is below the peak by more than 0.2 max( |Gpeak |, 1) and the tracked actor has higher return, or (ii) final validation violations exceed one and the tracked return is at least the final return minus 0.2 max( |Gfinal |, 1) .Here Gpeak = max u Gu is the maximum over all validation checkpoints, including ineligible ones, and Gfinal is the final-checkpoint mean. The critics and world model are not selected. Final testing uses 20 fixed seeds disjoint from training and validation, exactly once after any actor restoration.

## F EXACT ENVIRONMENT TRANSFORMATIONS 

All modifications below are applied identically to training, validation, and test instances of the corresponding domain. In the HalfCheetah gravity environment, the simulator’s vertical gravity is set to −19 .62 m s −2 and the target retains the MuJoCo default −9.81 m s −2. Both members continue the episode when qz > 0.2 and subtract 10[max(0 , q z − 0.2)] 2 from the standard HalfCheetah reward. For Friction Walker, every sliding, torsional, and rolling geom-friction coefficient is multiplied by 0.3 in the simulator; the target retains the default coefficients. Both domains subtract 10[max(0 , s (0) −

1.25)] 2 from the standard Walker2d reward, where s(0) is the root-height observation. For Ant actuator failure, target actions 4–7 are set to zero before the physics step; the simulator applies all eight actions. Both domains retain the standard Ant health termination, add a rear-wall termination when the world-frame base position falls below −3 along the first horizontal coordinate, and set the wall-step reward to zero. Away from the wall, the project-specific reward is planar Euclidean distance from the origin plus 1, minus 0.5 times the squared norm of the applied action. The soft cap further subtracts [max(0 , |vx| − 1.5)] 2. Thus disabled target channels incur no control cost after they are zeroed. The reported Ant method has an empty deterministic predicate set; these environment rewards and terminations still apply.
