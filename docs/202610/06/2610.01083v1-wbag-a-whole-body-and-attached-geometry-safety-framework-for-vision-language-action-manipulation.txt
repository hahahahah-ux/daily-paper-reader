# WBAG: A Whole-Body and Attached-Geometry Safety Framework for Vision-Language-Action Manipulation 

Samuel Zhen 1, Siwon Jo 2, Yanze Zhang 3, and Wenhao Luo 1

Abstract — Vision-language-action (VLA) policies have demonstrated impressive capabilities in generalizable robotic manipulation, but their deployment in the real world remains challenging due to potential collisions involving different parts of the robot, manipulated objects, and the surrounding environment. Existing inference-time VLA safety frameworks typically rely on simplified end-effector-centered representations that do not explicitly model the full articulated robot and attached-object geometry. In this paper, we present WBAG, a safety framework that models the robot’s whole-body and grasp-dependent attached geometry. WBAG constructs a grasp-conditioned safe set that adapts the protected geometry as objects are grasped, then converts this evolving geometry into differentiable CBF constraints that minimally modify the VLA’s native six-dimensional operational-space action for collision avoidance across robot, scene, and attached geometry. On the SafeLIBERO benchmark, a variant of LIBERO augmented with obstacles for safety evaluation, WBAG achieves the best overall safety and safe task success among the evaluated methods under a scene-level safety evaluator that monitors all eligible non-task objects, reaching 97.38% aggregate Scene Safety and 59.38% Safe Success. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Vision-language-action (VLA) models map visual obser-vations and language instructions directly to robot actions across diverse manipulation tasks [1], but their task-level generalization does not provide an explicit geometric safety model to consistently safeguard robot motions. In cluttered environments, a VLA action may move the end effector toward the target while causing an upstream link, wrist, grip-per, or manipulated object to collide. The relevant collision geometry spans the articulated robot and changes when the robot grasps an object. Recent benchmark evaluations further show that task completion alone can obscure physical safety violations during manipulation [2], [3]. Control-theoretic safety methods, particularly control bar-rier functions (CBFs), provide a principled mechanism for enforcing collision avoidance during robotic control [4]. CBFs constrain control inputs to maintain forward invariance of a prescribed safe set, but their effectiveness depends on how that set is represented. For geometric collision avoid-ance, the safety function must capture separation between the protected robot geometry and surrounding obstacles.

The geometric representation therefore determines which collision modes can be enforced. Recent work on safety-critical control with VLA-based policies [5]–[9] spans both training-time and inference-time interventions. SafeVLA [5] incorporates safety constraints during policy optimization through constrained reinforce-ment learning. At inference time, AEGIS [6] combines perception-derived geometric information with CBF con-straints to filter a pretrained VLA, while KNOWS [7] com-bines VLA-based target identification with online obstacle tracking. Other methods enforce safety directly within flow-matching action generation. Recent work in [8] apply pre-dictive trajectory-level barrier constraints through minimum-norm corrections during iterative denoising, while [9] modify the flow-matching dynamics using an aggregate CBF over the generated action chunk. However, existing inference-time safety frameworks typically enforce collision avoid-ance using simplified end-effector-centered geometry [6]–[8]. Such end-effector-centered representations do not explicitly model the full articulated robot or the rigid geometry of an attached object: the end effector may remain separated while an upstream link, wrist, or attached geometry collides with an obstacle. Geometric methods such as Bernstein-polynomial signed distance fields (BP-SDFs) [10] provide smooth, differen-arXiv:2610.01083v1 [cs.RO] 1 Oct 2026 tiable representations of object geometry, enabling efficient distance queries and gradients that are well suited for optimization-based control. For articulated robots, BP-SDFs can represent individual links, whose poses are determined by forward kinematics as a function of robot configuration. Recent work [11] employs BP-SDFs to construct geometry-aware CBFs from the minimum distance between robot and obstacle geometries and derive corresponding safety constraints on the robot control input. Applying such ge-ometric safety constraints to VLA manipulation requires connecting perception, manipulation state, and the policy’s native action interface. In our setting, scene and target geometry are reconstructed from RGB-D observations rather than assumed known, while the protected geometry changes when the target becomes rigidly attached after grasping. At the same time, the resulting link- and object-level clearances are functions of robot configuration, whereas the frozen VLA produces six-dimensional operational-space actions. These factors must therefore be coupled so that perception-derived, grasp-conditioned geometric constraints can be enforced directly on the VLA action without retraining the VLA or modifying the operational-space controller. In this paper, we introduce WBAG , a whole-body and attached-geometry safety framework for pretrained VLA policies. WBAG reconstructs scene and target geometry from RGB-D observations and constructs a grasp-conditioned safe set : before grasping, it protects the articulated robot; after grasping, the protected set expands to include the rigidly attached object. We represent robot, scene, and at-tached geometry with differentiable BP-SDFs and enforce the resulting CBF constraints directly on the VLA’s native six-dimensional operational-space action. WBAG minimally modifies the policy output while accounting for collisions involving the whole robot and attached geometry, without retraining the underlying VLA or modifying the operational-space controller. We evaluate WBAG on SafeLIBERO using a scene-level safety evaluator that monitors all eligible non-task scene objects. WBAG achieves the highest aggregate Scene Safety and Safe Success among the evaluated con-figurations, reaching 97 .38% and 59 .38% , respectively. Section III defines a grasp-conditioned geometric barrier that assumes exact continuous closest-point evaluation be-tween each protected body and obstacle. WBAG instantiates this formulation as the perception-to-control safety pipeline shown in Fig. 2. At episode initializa-tion, multi-view RGB-D observations are used to reconstruct scene and target geometry, while robot-link BP-SDFs are precomputed offline. During execution, the protected set is updated when a grasp is confirmed and the attached-object geometry is propagated with the robot hand. The ideal closest-point barriers are approximated online using finite local contact search, and the resulting geometric constraints are mapped through a local action-to-motion model to the VLA’s six-dimensional operational-space command. WBAG then solves a quadratic program (QP) that minimally mod-ifies the nominal VLA action while satisfying the grasp-conditioned geometric CBF constraints. 

A. Perception-derived Scene and Target Geometry 

To instantiate the geometric barriers in Section III, WBAG combines precomputed robot geometry with perception-derived scene and target geometry. Robot-link BP-SDFs are precomputed offline from collision meshes using axis-wise second-difference constraints on the Bernstein coefficients to promote convex BP-SDF level sets. At episode initialization, scene and target geometry are reconstructed from multi-view RGB-D observations, as shown in Fig. 2. SAM3 [12] provides object masks for the camera observations, and the corresponding masked depth observations are fused into world-frame point clouds. Task-role information identifies the target, goal, and support objects and determines which reconstructed components are excluded from the obstacle set. For each reconstructed object, including the task target, we construct a convex hull of the observed points and fit a BP-SDF using the same convexity-promoting constraints. The retained non-task objects define the scene obstacle set 

O, while the target BP-SDF is retained for incorporation into the protected set after grasping.

A. Evaluation Setup 

We evaluate on SafeLIBERO [6], a safety oriented ex-tension of LIBERO [16] for tabletop manipulation. Safe-LIBERO contains 16 tasks drawn from the LIBERO Goal, Long, Object, and Spatial suites, with four tasks from each suite. The Spatial tasks vary the spatial arrangement of the manipulated objects, the Object tasks vary which object must be transported, the Goal tasks vary the desired manipula-tion outcome, and the Long tasks require longer sequential interactions involving multiple objects. Across these suites, the robot performs pickup, transport, placement, articulated interactions such as opening a drawer, and multi object manipulation. SafeLIBERO augments each task with two safety obstacle placements. In Level I, the designated obstacle is placed near the target object. In Level II, it is placed farther from the target but along the robot’s motion path. Each task and safety level contains 50 randomized episodes, yielding 1,600 episodes in total. Following SafeLIBERO’s displacement based safety proxy [6], we evaluate all methods using a scene evaluator that monitors every eligible scene body not required for task contact. An episode is unsafe if any such body is displaced by more than 0.01 m from its settled initial position pi(0) :

∥pi(t) − pi(0) ∥1 > 0.01 m . (30) Target objects, goals, and required support objects are ex-cluded because contact with them may be necessary for task completion. We report three episode level metrics. Scene Safety holds when no evaluated body violates Eq. (30). Safe Success

B. Benchmark Performance 

Table II reports task and safety performance on Safe-LIBERO. Scene Safety increases from 70.87% for AEGIS baseline to 72.94% for SAM3 EEF MVEE , 82.75% for SAM3 Scene EEF MVEE , and 97.38% for full WBAG. Extending obstacle coverage from SAM3 EEF MVEE to SAM3 Scene EEF MVEE improves Scene Safety, but reduces Safe Success from 55.56% to 46.25%. WBAG further increases Scene Safety to 97.38% while achieving 59.38% Safe Success and reducing Unsafe Success to 0.68%. These results show that broader scene coverage alone improves safety at the cost of task completion, while WBAG improves safety without the same loss in Safe Success. Across individual suites, WBAG achieves the highest Scene Safety and lowest Unsafe Success in all four suites. WBAG also achieves the highest Safe Success on Long and Object, while the EEF MVEE configurations achieve the highest Safe Success on Goal and Spatial. This variation indicates that the effect of the safety filter on task completion depends on the manipulation tasks within each suite. 

C. Ablation and Qualitative Analysis 

The WBAG ablations in Table II isolate the effects of whole body and attached object protection. Comparing WBAG with WBAG EEF only + attached geometry shows that whole body protection improves both Scene Safety and Safe Success. Comparing WBAG with WBAG without at-tached geometry shows an additional benefit from explicitly protecting the grasped object. Together, these comparisons show that whole body and attached geometry modeling provide complementary benefits for collision avoidance. Figure 5 illustrates the corresponding geometric failure modes. Incomplete scene coverage can leave non task objects unconstrained. End effector only protection can remain satis-fied while an upstream robot link collides with an obstacle. A handcrafted end effector proxy can also fail to capture the shape and grasp relative pose of the manipulated object, allowing collisions involving attached geometry.

In this paper, we proposed WBAG, an inference-time safety framework for VLA manipulation that explicitly mod-els whole-body and grasp-conditioned attached geometry. WBAG constructs BP-SDF clearance barriers over a grasp-conditioned protected set and enforces the resulting geomet-ric CBF constraints directly on the VLA’s native operational-space action, without retraining the VLA or modifying the operational-space controller. On SafeLIBERO, WBAG achieves 97.38% Scene Safety and 59.38% Safe Success. Controlled ablations show com-plementary benefits from whole-body and attached-geometry protection, while the complete filter requires 26.76 ms per filtering step on average. Together, these results support explicitly modeling the full robot–attached-object system rather than relying on end-effector-centered safety geometry for runtime VLA manipulation. The fixed-mode forward-invariance result applies to the ideal geometric barrier, whereas the deployed framework uses finite contact search, a local action-to-motion ap-proximation, and QP slack. Our evaluation also relies on simulator-provided task-role identities and does not explicitly model uncertainty in reconstructed or attached geometry. Future work will investigate uncertainty-aware geometry and task-role inference, dynamic obstacles, policy-aware filtering that better preserves task completion, with validation in the real world.
